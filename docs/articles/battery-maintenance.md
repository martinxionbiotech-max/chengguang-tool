# Car Battery Maintenance Guide

## How to Clean Battery Terminals

Corrosion (white/blue powder) increases resistance, causing slow cranking and charging problems.

**What you need:** Baking soda, water, wire brush, petroleum jelly

**Steps:**
1. Disconnect negative (−) first, then positive (+)
2. Mix 1 tablespoon baking soda with 1 cup water
3. Apply paste to terminals — it will fizz as it neutralizes acid
4. Scrub with wire brush until terminals are shiny
5. Rinse with clean water, dry thoroughly
6. Apply thin coat of petroleum jelly to prevent future corrosion
7. Reconnect positive (+) first, then negative (−)

## How to Check Water Levels (Flooded Batteries Only)

!!! danger "Sealed batteries"
    Do NOT attempt to open sealed (AGM/maintenance-free) batteries. They are designed to be closed and opening them will damage the battery.

1. Remove vent caps
2. Check each cell — electrolyte should cover plates by ~5mm
3. If low, add **distilled water only** (not tap water)
4. Do not overfill — leave space for expansion
5. Replace vent caps

**How often:** Monthly in hot climates, every 3 months in temperate.

## Preventing Battery Drain

Common causes of parasitic drain:

| Cause | Drain (Amps) | Solution |
|-------|:---:|------|
| Interior light left on | 0.5–2A | Check before leaving vehicle |
| Aftermarket alarm/stereo | 0.1–0.5A | Professional installation |
| Faulty alternator diode | 0.5–3A | Replace alternator |
| Glove box/trunk light stuck | 0.5–1A | Check and fix switch |
| OBD-II device plugged in | 0.05–0.1A | Unplug when not driving |

**Normal parasitic drain:** 25–50 mA (0.025–0.050A) for modern vehicles with computers and alarms.

## Seasonal Battery Care

### Summer Preparation (Hot Climates)

- Check water levels weekly (flooded batteries)
- Park in shade whenever possible
- Clean terminals — heat accelerates corrosion
- Test CCA — catch degradation before the battery fails in heat

### Winter Preparation (Cold Climates)

- Test CCA in autumn — replace if below 70% of rating
- Keep battery fully charged (12.6V+) — a discharged battery freezes
- Use a battery blanket or insulator in extreme cold (<-20°C)
- Start the engine and let it run every few days if vehicle sits idle

## When to Use a Battery Charger

| Situation | Charger Type | Duration |
|-----------|-------------|:---:|
| Battery <12.2V | Smart charger (2–10A) | 4–8 hours |
| Battery deeply discharged | Smart charger (2A trickle) | 12–24 hours |
| Vehicle sits >2 weeks | Battery maintainer/tender | Continuous |
| Jump-started and need full charge | Smart charger (10A) | 2–4 hours driving or charging |

## Signs Your Battery Needs Replacement

| Sign | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Slow cranking | CCA is declining |
| Clicking sound when starting | Not enough current to engage starter |
| Swollen/bloated case | Internal failure — replace immediately |
| Rotten egg smell | Overcharging — sulfuric acid leaking |
| Battery >4 years old | End of expected life |
| Need frequent jump-starts | Battery cannot hold charge |
| Headlights dim at idle | Battery or alternator issue |

## FAQ

### Can I jump-start a completely dead battery?
Yes, but a completely dead battery (below 10.5V) may be internally damaged. After jump-starting, drive for 30+ minutes or use a charger. Test the battery afterward.

### How long can a car sit before the battery dies?
A healthy battery in a modern car can sit **2–4 weeks** before discharge from parasitic drain becomes problematic. Older cars (no computers) can sit **2–3 months**. Use a battery maintainer for longer storage.

### Does idling charge the battery?
Yes, but slowly. At idle, the alternator produces ~30–50% of its rated output. Driving at highway speeds charges much faster.

### Should I disconnect the battery when storing my car?
Yes, for storage longer than 2 weeks. Disconnect the negative terminal. This eliminates parasitic drain. Note that you may need to reset radio presets and clock.

### What is battery sulfation?
Sulfation occurs when lead sulfate crystals form on the battery plates during discharge. If the battery remains discharged, these crystals harden and reduce capacity permanently. Some smart chargers have a "desulfation" mode that attempts to reverse this.
